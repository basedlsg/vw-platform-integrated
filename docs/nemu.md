# Nemu — Finance Decision Board

**Live at:** https://nemu.gamechangerlabs.io
**Repo:** https://github.com/basedlsg/vw-platform-integrated
**Demo data:** VW Group historical financials (Q1 2022 → Q4 2024) and Q1 2025 market events

---

## What it is, in one paragraph

Nemu is a one-page decision tool for a corporate finance team. It connects the news the team reads to the numbers they're responsible for, and logs every decision in between. An analyst opens Nemu in the morning, sees a calm summary of what's going on, scans a short list of incoming events ("BYD just cut prices in China"), opens the ones that matter, asks colleagues to verify, drafts a structured response, sends it to a manager, and either gets it approved (in which case planning targets actually move) or shelved. Every step is recorded with who did what, when, and what changed as a result.

It's the spreadsheet-plus-Slack-plus-meeting-notes workflow most CFO offices run today, compressed into one screen with a real audit trail underneath.

---

## Who it's for

Built for finance decision makers at a global automaker. The current build runs on VW Group data, but the shape of the product applies to any company with global market exposure, EV transition pressure, regulatory risk, and quarterly KPIs to defend.

Three roles use the same screen, with slightly different views:

- **FP&A analysts** — the daily user. Reads the briefing, opens signals, talks to colleagues, submits scenarios up to a manager.
- **Finance managers** — opens scenarios that have been escalated to them, reviews the proposed change, approves or rejects.
- **CFO office** — uses the decision log and KPI strip in leadership reviews; sees what the team has been deciding and why.

---

## The core flow

The product is built around one explicit workflow:

```
Signal → Scenario → Discussion → Approval → Ripple → Ledger
```

Read it as: a thing happens in the world (Signal), the team frames a structured response to it (Scenario), the right colleagues confirm or push back (Discussion), a manager approves or rejects (Approval), if approved the planning numbers actually update (Ripple), and the whole sequence is recorded permanently (Ledger).

That flow is the entire product. Every screen, every button, every API exists to move a signal along this path.

## Walking through the home screen, top to bottom

### 1. Briefing — "This morning"

Two to four calm sentences at the very top of the page. Plain prose, no bullets. Generated by Meta's Llama-4-Maverick model and grounded in actual data: the current operating margin, cash conversion, and EV-share values, plus the active signal queue.

A typical briefing reads like:

> *"The current operating margin is 3.1%, cash conversion is 61%, and EV market share is 10.8%, indicating a stable overall picture. A potential pressure point is the pending US Senate vote on a 25% import tariff. The XPENG co-developed platform A-sample validation is a stabilizing factor. The delayed PowerCo Salzgitter cell ramp is one thing to watch."*

The briefing regenerates whenever a KPI value changes or a signal is acted on, so it stays current. It doesn't make up numbers — every figure you see in it came from the database.

### 2. Signal feed

Three to five cards listing what's currently in the queue. Each card is deliberately minimal:

- **Title** of the event
- **Touches:** the strategy pillars it affects (e.g. *China · EV · Margin*)
- **May affect:** the KPIs and risks it could move (e.g. *EV market share · Operating margin*)

Nothing else — no urgency badges, no source-date footers, no commitment-color labels. Cards are ordered by recency. Click any card to open the workspace.

The feed currently shows:

- BYD cuts EV prices in China (CAAM / Handelsblatt, Jan 2025)
- US 25% import tariff moving to Senate vote (USTR / Reuters, Feb 2025)
- XPENG co-developed platform reaches A-sample validation (VW IR / CnEVPost, Feb 2025)
- EU BEV share rises to 15.4% — VW down 0.4pp (ACEA / Automotive News, Mar 2025)
- PowerCo Salzgitter cell ramp delayed 6 weeks (Handelsblatt, Mar 2025)

These are real Q1 2025 events, hand-curated for the demo. Production replaces curation with automatic ingestion (see "Where the data comes from" below).

### 3. Approval queue (only appears when there's something to approve)

If any signals have been submitted up to a manager, a small section appears between the feed and the KPI strip showing them under **"Waiting on a decision"**, split into:

- **Needs your call** (orange) — items in `AWAITING_APPROVAL`
- **Still being verified** (purple) — items in `IN_DISCUSSION`

Click a row to open the same workspace panel, but in **manager mode** with Approve / Reject / Watch as the action bar instead of the analyst's chip row.

### 4. KPI strip — three live numbers

Operating margin, cash conversion, EV market share. Each shows the current value, three-year quarterly trend chart, target, and a one-line variance note ("2.9pp below target"). Click any one to open a deeper view with full source history.

These charts are driven by actual events in the database. When a manager approves a scenario that lowers a target, the corresponding KPI immediately reflects the new value here.

### 5. Decision log — the audit trail

A quiet table at the bottom of the page. One row per non-pending decision: signal, status (APPROVED / REJECTED / DISMISSED), discussion-message count, ripple summary (what changed if approved), and date. Click any row to expand the full event timeline of that proposal — every status change, every verification message, every cascaded KPI update.

The log is the SOX/J-SOX audit artifact. "Who approved the BEV share target change to 10.8%, against what evidence, on what date" is a one-click answer.

## Opening a signal — the workspace panel

When a card is clicked, a side drawer slides in from the right. It has three tabs.

### Overview

Four short blocks of plain text:

- **What happened** — the signal description, verbatim from the source.
- **Why it matters** — one to two sentences from Llama, grounded in the signal's pillars and affected KPIs, and (since the latest release) referencing past decisions when relevant. Example: *"Last time a similar China-pricing signal came up, the team approved a target cut to 10.8% — worth tracking whether this further compounds that."*
- **What it could affect** — the colored pillar tags and the KPI / risk labels listed plainly.
- **Suggested change** — the structured "if approved, change KPI X to Y" payload attached to the signal, plus the disclaimer *"Numbers do not change until a manager approves."*

A source line at the bottom shows where the signal came from (publication + date).

### Discuss

A clean message thread. Type a question, click Send, the message goes into the thread. Click "Simulate a reply" and Llama responds in the role of the appropriate stakeholder (China Finance for CHINA pillar, Trade & Tariff Desk for REGULATORY, EV Strategy for EV, etc.) — referencing real signal data when useful, honest about gaps when it doesn't.

Each message is recorded as a `ScenarioVerificationEvent`, so the discussion is part of the audit log forever. When the scenario gets approved later, the manager (and any auditor) can replay exactly who said what to inform the decision.

### Simulate

A single slider with one input and one number out. For the BYD signal: drag the "Additional BYD price gap (pp)" slider, see the estimated impact on BEV share in two quarters update live. The math is `outputBase + (input − inputDefault) × outputCoefficient` — deliberately simple, an order-of-magnitude estimate, not a forecast. There's a "Customize…" affordance for a future multi-input view.

### The action bar

Five chips along the bottom in analyst mode: **Watch** / **Discuss** / **Simulate** / **Submit for approval** / **Dismiss**. Clicking any of them moves the signal to a new lifecycle state and writes a status-change event. Critically, *only* "Submit for approval" leads to the manager flow, and *only* a manager's "Approve" click commits the actual KPI / risk change. Everything else is logging-only — no planning numbers move without explicit approval.

In manager mode the chip row is replaced by **Approve scenario** / **Watch** / **Reject**.

## The AI narration layer

Three places use Llama, all anchored to real database state:

1. **Briefing** ("This morning") — 2-4 sentences, regenerates on state change.
2. **"Why it matters"** — 1-2 sentences when a signal is opened.
3. **Discuss "Simulate a reply"** — role-cast as the right stakeholder, replies to the latest analyst question.

What Llama is and isn't:

- **Is**: a narrator. It writes prose grounded in data the system passes it, in a calm professional tone.
- **Isn't**: a decision-maker. It never picks the new KPI target (that comes from the signal's `proposedStateChange` payload, which is hand-authored). It never approves a scenario. It never sends a message that wasn't requested by a button click.
- **Constrained**: every prompt explicitly asks it to answer "ONLY from the data the user provides," reference specific numbers, write calmly, no markdown. The system prompt casts it as "a senior VW Group financial analyst" so the analytical register stays consistent.

Currently powered by `Llama-4-Maverick-17B-128E-Instruct-FP8` via Meta's official Llama API at `api.llama.com/v1`. The model is configurable through env vars; swap in any other Llama variant (Scout, 3.3-70B, 3.3-8B) by changing one line.

If the AI endpoint is unavailable, the surfaces simply render blank rather than fall back to fake-filled prose. The product still works without AI — every action button, every state transition, every cascade is independent of the narration layer.

## Institutional memory — the recall feature

Most recently added. Today when the AI writes the briefing or "why it matters," it sees the current snapshot. It does *not* see what the team decided last quarter when a similar signal came in. So the recall feature was built.

When any AI surface fires, it now first calls a `/api/memory/recall` endpoint that:

- Walks the closed-state proposal history (APPROVED / REJECTED / DISMISSED)
- Scores each past decision against the current signal by overlap on strategy pillars and affected KPIs
- Weights APPROVED higher than REJECTED higher than DISMISSED (approved decisions are the most informative)
- Decays by recency (90-day half-life — recent decisions weigh more than year-old ones)
- Returns the top 3-5 most relevant past decisions with their ripple summaries and verification counts

That block of context gets injected into the prompt, so the narration can reference what's already been decided. The system isn't generating fake memory — every line in the recall block is a real event from the database, with a real timestamp and a real outcome.

The implementation is deliberately small: one SQL scan over the events table, no embeddings, no vector store, no graph database. When proposal volume outgrows linear scan (months to years away), swap the score function for pgvector cosine similarity on stored title+description embeddings. Same recall API, drop-in replacement.

## Where the data comes from

Three categories of data flow through Nemu:

### Financial numbers (KPIs)

- **Today**: a curated JSON file with 36 historical data points — operating margin, cash conversion, and EV share for every quarter from Q1 2022 to Q4 2024. Sourced from VW Group's public Annual Reports and Half-Year Reports. A human read the financials and typed them in.
- **Production**: an overnight script pulls actuals from SAP via the standard OData API and writes them to the same `events` table. The dashboard charts are unchanged; only the source of the values is different. This is two days of integration work plus access to the right SAP module.

### Market signals

- **Today**: six items hand-typed from real Q1 2025 events. Each one is a structured event in the database with a title, description, source, the strategy pillars it touches, and an attached "proposed change" payload describing what should happen if approved.
- **Production**: a scheduled job polls Reuters, Handelsblatt, ACEA filings, USTR markup feeds, SEC EDGAR, GDELT, and other sources every 15 minutes. A small classification step (a local LLM call, or a fine-tuned scoring model) decides whether the item is relevant to VW's current strategy pillars. If yes, it gets written into the same event store as `AgentProposalCreatedEvent`. The cards on screen are unchanged. The architecture supports this today via a `/api/ingest/events` endpoint that just needs the scheduler wired up.

### What each signal "means" (the proposed change)

- **Today**: every signal carries a hand-authored `proposedStateChange` payload — for the BYD signal, that's `{ kpis: { KPI_BEV_SHARE: { target: 10.8 } } }`. The 10.8 was typed by a human. Llama explains the change in plain English; it does not derive the number.
- **Production**: a small driver tree (price gap × volume × elasticity → margin impact) replaces the hand-authored target. The AI narration layer is unchanged — it still explains what the change means, but the change itself comes from a model. This is the meaningful step from "demo" to "decision tool."

## Data model — events, not snapshots

Underneath the UI, the entire system is event-sourced. Nothing is updated in place; every change is a new immutable row in the `events` table with a timestamp and source.

The five event types:

- `KpiValueUpdatedEvent` — a KPI value was reset (either by a quarterly actual coming in, or by an approved scenario cascading)
- `RiskThresholdSetEvent` — a risk's threshold or impact level was changed
- `AgentProposalCreatedEvent` — a new signal arrived
- `ProposalStatusChangedEvent` — a signal moved through the workflow (PENDING → WATCHING → IN_DISCUSSION → AWAITING_APPROVAL → APPROVED / REJECTED / DISMISSED)
- `ScenarioVerificationEvent` — a discussion message was posted

When the dashboard loads, the system reads every event for every entity, runs them through a deterministic *materializer* (a pure function that replays events into the current state), and renders the result. To change history, you don't edit a row — you write a new event. The audit trail is a property of the architecture, not a feature bolted on.

This is what makes the AI variance commentary trustworthy: when Llama writes "operating margin moved from 8.3% to 3.1%," every value in that prose is one specific event in the database with a specific source citation attached.

## What the product does today

Open it in the morning. Read the briefing. Scan the five signal cards. Click into the BYD signal. Read the AI explanation. Drag the simulate slider, see the rough impact. Type a question to China Finance, send it. Click Simulate-a-reply, get a contextual answer. Submit for approval. Switch to manager view by clicking the inbox row. Approve. Watch the BEV share target update on the dashboard. See the decision land in the log. Five minutes, end-to-end.

The complete workflow runs against a real Postgres database (Supabase) with real audit-log events. Llama (Meta-hosted) writes the narration. The dev environment is a single Next.js app on Vercel, accessible at https://nemu.gamechangerlabs.io.

## What it doesn't do yet

Honest list:

1. **Signal ingestion is hand-curated.** The six signals were typed in. RSS / EDGAR / GDELT scheduler is the next step.
2. **Financial numbers are seeded historicals.** Real-time SAP / Workday / Oracle integration is unwired.
3. **The "what should change" payload is hand-authored.** A driver-tree model that derives `target` from real elasticities exists nowhere in the codebase yet.
4. **No push alerts.** The team has to open Nemu to see what changed. A 7am email or Slack DM with the briefing is straightforward to add — needs Slack/email credentials and a cron.
5. **No multi-user authentication.** Anyone with the URL can act as analyst or manager. SSO + role-based access is the first thing a real procurement review will ask for.
6. **No export to board deck.** The decision log can't currently render to PowerPoint or PDF. Useful for monthly leadership reviews. Probably one day of work.
7. **Discuss is not actually wired to Slack.** "Simulate a reply" is a Llama-generated stakeholder response, not a real message from a real person. A 200-line Slack adapter that POSTs real `#vw-china-finance` messages into the discuss endpoint would close this gap without rewriting anything.

## Tech stack (briefly)

- **Frontend / backend**: Next.js 16 (App Router) + React 19 + TypeScript, deployed on Vercel
- **Database**: Supabase Postgres (single `events` table with a unique index on `(entity_id, entity_type, sequence)`)
- **State**: Zustand with Immer + persist middleware (versioned, so schema changes invalidate stale browser caches)
- **AI**: Meta Llama API (`api.llama.com/v1`), `Llama-4-Maverick-17B-128E-Instruct-FP8` by default
- **Charts**: Recharts
- **Styling**: Tailwind v4 + custom CSS variables (theme-aware: dark + light mode)
- **i18n**: full bilingual EN / ZH out of the box, parity-checked at build time

The whole runtime fits in one Next.js app and one Supabase project. No Kafka, no microservices, no separate cache layer, no message bus. Operationally it's small enough that one engineer can reason about the whole system.
